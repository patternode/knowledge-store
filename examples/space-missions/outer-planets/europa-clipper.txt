BRIEFING NOTE: EUROPA CLIPPER
Prepared for internal circulation, knowledge-store demo corpus

OVERVIEW
Europa Clipper is a NASA mission built and managed by the Jet Propulsion Laboratory, with the
Applied Physics Laboratory contributing to spacecraft development, sent to conduct detailed
reconnaissance of Jupiter's icy moon Europa. It launched on October 14, 2024, from Kennedy
Space Center aboard a Falcon Heavy rocket, making it one of the larger planetary spacecraft
NASA has built, with solar arrays wide enough to span a basketball court, a necessity given how
little sunlight reaches Jupiter's distance from the Sun.

WHY EUROPA
Interest in Europa dates back to the Galileo mission of the 1990s, which found strong
circumstantial evidence, based on magnetic field measurements, for a global liquid water ocean
beneath Europa's ice shell. Europa Clipper does not land or search for life directly; instead
it is designed to determine whether the ocean's environment could plausibly support it, by
studying the ice shell's thickness, the ocean beneath, and the moon's composition and geology.

TRAJECTORY AND INSTRUMENTS
Rather than orbit Europa directly, which would expose the spacecraft to Jupiter's intense
radiation belts, Europa Clipper is designed to orbit Jupiter and make dozens of close flybys of
Europa, ducking in and out of the radiation environment. The mission uses gravity assists,
including a flyby of Mars, on its way to Jupiter, arriving in 2030. Instruments include an
ice-penetrating radar (REASON), a magnetometer to characterize the ocean, thermal and mapping
cameras, and a mass spectrometer to analyze any thin atmosphere or plume material.

RELATION TO OTHER MISSIONS
Europa Clipper follows in the footsteps of Galileo's flybys and complements ESA's earlier
JUICE mission (Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer), launched in 2023, which focuses on Ganymede and
Callisto as well as Europa. Between the two missions, Jupiter's three large icy moons are all
under close study for the first time.

STATUS
Europa Clipper is in transit as of the mid-2020s, with Europa flybys expected to begin after
Jupiter orbit insertion later in the decade.
