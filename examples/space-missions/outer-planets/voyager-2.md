# Voyager 2

Voyager 2 was launched by NASA on August 20, 1977, from Cape Canaveral aboard a Titan IIIE
launch vehicle, about sixteen days before its sibling craft Voyager 1. Both probes were
designed and are still operated by the Jet Propulsion Laboratory. Voyager 2 followed a slower,
more roundabout trajectory that happened to line up with a rare planetary alignment, letting
mission planners send it on to Uranus and Neptune after its encounters with Jupiter and Saturn.

## The Grand Tour

Voyager 2 is, to date, the only spacecraft to have visited Uranus and Neptune. It flew past
Jupiter in 1979, Saturn in 1981, Uranus in January 1986, and Neptune in August 1989, using a
gravity assist at each planet to reach the next. At Uranus it discovered ten new moons and
studied the planet's oddly tilted magnetic field. At Neptune, Voyager 2 found the Great Dark
Spot, a large storm system, and returned the first close images of the moon Triton, showing
active geyser-like plumes of nitrogen.

## Instruments and design

Like Voyager 1, the spacecraft carries a wide-angle and narrow-angle camera system, an
infrared spectrometer, an ultraviolet spectrometer, a magnetometer, and plasma and cosmic ray
detectors. Both Voyagers are powered by radioisotope thermoelectric generators rather than
solar panels, a necessity given how faint sunlight becomes at Neptune's distance from the Sun.
Each probe also carries an identical Golden Record, assembled under the direction of
astronomer Carl Sagan, containing greetings in dozens of languages and a selection of music.

## Interstellar space

Voyager 2 crossed the heliopause in November 2018, six years after Voyager 1, becoming the
second human-made object to enter interstellar space, and the first to carry a working plasma
instrument across the boundary, since that instrument on Voyager 1 had already failed. The two
Voyagers left the heliosphere at different points, giving scientists two independent samples of
the boundary region. Mission controllers continue to receive a faint signal from Voyager 2
through the Deep Space Network, coordinating carefully with Voyager 1's team since both
missions are managed together and share a shrinking pool of onboard power for the decade ahead.
