# James Webb Space Telescope

The James Webb Space Telescope, widely known as JWST or simply Webb, is a large infrared
observatory developed by NASA in partnership with the European Space Agency (ESA) and the
Canadian Space Agency (CSA). It is named after James E. Webb, who led NASA during much of the
Apollo era, and it is considered the successor to the Hubble Space Telescope, though it
observes primarily in infrared light rather than visible light.

## Launch and destination

Webb launched on December 25, 2021, from the Guiana Space Centre in Kourou, French Guiana,
aboard an Ariane 5 rocket provided by ESA. Unlike Hubble, which orbits Earth at low altitude,
Webb travels to a gravitationally stable point called the second Sun-Earth Lagrange point (L2),
about 1.5 million kilometers from Earth, where it can keep its sunshield constantly between
itself and the Sun, Earth, and Moon.

## A folded telescope

Webb's 6.5-meter primary mirror, made of eighteen gold-coated beryllium segments, was too
large to fit in any rocket fully assembled, so the telescope launched folded up and unfurled
itself over several weeks, including a tennis-court-sized, five-layer sunshield that had to
unfold without tearing or snagging, a sequence engineers considered one of the mission's
riskiest steps.

## Instruments

Webb carries four main instruments: NIRCam (Near-Infrared Camera), NIRSpec (Near-Infrared
Spectrograph), built with ESA, MIRI (Mid-Infrared Instrument), built with a European
consortium, and NIRISS (Near-Infrared Imager and Slitless Spectrograph), contributed by the
Canadian Space Agency. Together they let Webb see faint, distant, and dust-obscured objects
that visible-light telescopes cannot.

## Early results

Since beginning science operations in mid-2022, Webb has captured extremely distant galaxies
seen not long after the early universe, detailed views of star-forming regions, and detected
chemical signatures, including carbon dioxide and other molecules, in the atmospheres of
planets orbiting other stars. Webb has also observed objects closer to home, from Jupiter's
auroras to features on Mars, complementing missions like Perseverance operating on the
surface. Because Webb sits so far from Earth, it cannot be serviced by astronauts the way
Hubble once was, so its instruments and cooling systems were built with no planned repair
missions in mind.
