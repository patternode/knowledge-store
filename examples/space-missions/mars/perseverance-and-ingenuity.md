# Perseverance and Ingenuity

Perseverance is a car-sized NASA rover, built and operated by the Jet Propulsion Laboratory,
sent to search Mars for signs of ancient microbial life and to collect samples for eventual
return to Earth. It launched on July 30, 2020, from Cape Canaveral aboard an Atlas V rocket,
and it landed in Jezero Crater on February 18, 2021, using a landing sequence that included a
rocket-powered "sky crane," the same basic approach first used by Curiosity nearly a decade
earlier.

## Why Jezero Crater

Jezero Crater was chosen because orbital images show it once held a lake fed by a river delta,
making it a promising place to look for sediments that might preserve traces of ancient
microbial life, if Mars ever supported any. Perseverance carries a drill and a set of sealed
sample tubes, caching rock and soil cores on the Martian surface for a future joint NASA-ESA
mission to retrieve and bring back to Earth.

## A rover with a name, and a helicopter

The rover's name came from a nationwide student contest that NASA ran before launch; a
seventh-grader named Alexander Mather submitted the winning entry. Riding along underneath
Perseverance was a small, twin-rotor helicopter named Ingenuity, intended only as a technology
demonstration to see whether powered flight was possible in Mars's thin atmosphere, just about
one percent as dense as Earth's at sea level.

## Ingenuity's flights

Ingenuity made its first flight on April 19, 2021, the first powered, controlled flight by an
aircraft on another planet. Originally planned for about five flights, Ingenuity kept flying
for nearly three years, scouting terrain ahead of the rover, before a hard landing damaged its
rotor blades in January 2024 and ended its flying career.

## Instruments and ongoing work

Perseverance's instruments include Mastcam-Z, a zoomable stereo camera; SHERLOC, which searches
for organic compounds and minerals; and MOXIE, an experiment that demonstrated producing oxygen
from Mars's carbon dioxide atmosphere, a capability relevant to future human missions. As of
the mid-2020s, Perseverance continues exploring Jezero Crater and its surroundings, building on
groundwork laid by earlier Mars rovers including Curiosity.
