Curiosity

Curiosity is a NASA Mars rover, built and operated by the Jet Propulsion Laboratory as part of the Mars Science Laboratory program, sent to determine whether Mars ever had environmental conditions suitable for microbial life. It launched on November 26, 2011, from Cape Canaveral aboard an Atlas V rocket and landed inside Gale Crater on August 6, 2012.

The sky crane landing

Curiosity was too heavy to land using airbags, the method used by the earlier, smaller Mars rovers Spirit and Opportunity, so engineers designed a rocket-powered descent stage called a sky crane, which lowered the rover on cables to a soft landing before flying off to crash a safe distance away. The same landing method, refined further, was later reused for Perseverance in 2021.

Naming

Like Perseverance after it, Curiosity's name came from a student essay contest. Clara Ma, a sixth-grader from Kansas, submitted the winning entry, choosing "Curiosity" to capture the spirit of scientific exploration.

Exploring Mount Sharp

After studying Gale Crater's floor, Curiosity began climbing the layered lower slopes of Mount Sharp, a mountain of sediment rising from the crater floor whose rock layers record different chapters of Mars's climate history. Along the way its instruments, including the ChemCam laser spectrometer and the SAM (Sample Analysis at Mars) suite, detected organic molecules preserved in ancient mudstone and measured seasonal fluctuations in atmospheric methane, a gas that can be produced by either geological or biological processes.

Status

More than a decade after landing, Curiosity remains active, still climbing Mount Sharp and powered by a radioisotope thermoelectric generator rather than solar panels, which lets it keep working through Martian dust storms that have disabled solar-powered rovers in the past.
